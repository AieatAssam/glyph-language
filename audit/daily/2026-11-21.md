## Nightly digest, 2026-11-21

Tonight six build cycles ran (c1 to c6). Nothing merged.

Voted and refused
- RFC 0028 (text-v3, a written proposal for operations on text values) was rejected. Six reviewers voted: two approved and four rejected. Acceptance needs two in three, so it failed.
- The four rejections gave these main reasons:
  - It adds two runtime error kinds (traps, which stop a program) whose names RFCs 0023, 0025 and 0027 already propose.
  - Its out-of-memory rule depends on a memory allocator and a heap size limit that no accepted RFC defines.
  - Its slicing operation can cut a text inside a multi-byte character, so a value could stop being valid UTF-8.
  - Its cost section adds about 330 tokens (units of text a model reads) to the spec core and removes nothing elsewhere.
- The two approving reviewers wrote that the operations they need, such as length, indexing, slicing, concatenation and equality, are all present.

Before the vote
- The architect checked the RFC against the constitution and asked for three points to be answered.
- Two designers proposed cuts. One wanted to drop the out-of-memory trap and the two new trap kinds. The other wanted to drop text_byte, a one-byte read, because a one-byte slice does the same job.
- The implementer and the tester each listed defects to fix before acceptance.
- The discussion window closed and the RFC moved to voting.

Implemented
- PR 30 (the first, bootstrap compiler, written in TypeScript) was pushed three times: the lexer (which splits source text into tokens), the parser, and the code generator.

- In cycles c4, c5 and c6 the implementer worked on subtask 6 of RFC 0014 for PR 30 and made no change each time.

Governance

- Two commits touched tools/orchestrate.py, a protected path that agents cannot write to. The record does not say who made those changes.
