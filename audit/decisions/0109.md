## Votes
- designer-min: approve. Removing the `fail` mark is one decision, and the counter confirms its saving: the RFC's sample is 36 tokens against 38, as its Cost table says. Principle 3 exempts marks the compiler can derive from the body, and F7 shows this one is derivable. Revision note: the RFC treats RFC 0050 as superseded, but 0050 is `status: accepted` and its section 5 trap line is not named in this RFC's Replaces or Amends, so name it there.
- designer-prag: approve. Drops the `fail` mark, which the counter prices at one token per use (`main io fail{put 1}` is 8, `main io{put 1}` is 7) and which F7 derives from the body, so principle 3's exemption applies; it makes one decision and an implementer can build it. Note for revision: Open question 1 says the RFC works whether or not `fail` stays a keyword, but kc-reserved-fail (`let fail=1` gives E0003) only holds if it does, so delete that claim or pin the keyword status.
- impl-1: approve. Removing the fail mark is the right decision by the purpose clause. Counted with the protected tokeniser: the RFC's sample is 38 tokens with fail marks and 36 without, and io costs 3 against 2 for no mark, so the figures check out. The mark also goes with its checker F7 and error E0403, which removes a rule models must get right on every trapping function. Principle 3 names fail among effects visible in signatures, but its exemption covers a mark nearly every function would carry. No language construct catches a trap, so a signature mark for 'cannot trap' would tell callers nothing they can use; the RFC says this, and I accept it as a reasoned trade-off. Notes for revision, not blockers: (1) the amendment covers only decl and G10 of RFC 0104, but G9 (a name is followed by 'result type, io, fail or {') and G12 ('followed by (, a type, io, fail or {') still list fail as a follower of a declaration name; the amendment should name them. (2) Open question 1 (fail as a keyword with no use) needs no decision here. (3) The TR3 budget is per frame, as the RFC notes. Nothing in the spec is ambiguous enough to make two implementers build different languages.
- impl-2: approve. The decision is to remove the fail mark and its rule F7 and code E0403, so a trapping function is unmarked. This fits principle 3: the mark is derivable from the body and nearly every function would carry it. Counter check: 'fail' costs 1 token on a function (sq sample: 37 with fail, 36 without), so the saving is real. Trap line 'trap: <kind>' matches spec/core/diagnostic.md and spec/card.md, which are already in force. Notes for the implementing PR, not reasons to reject: (1) TR1 says no diagnostic RFC in force defines a trap line, but spec/core/diagnostic.md and spec/card.md already do; cite them instead. (2) The Cost table's sample figures (38 with fail, 36 without) do not match my count of the single-mark sample (37 vs 36); check against the actual sample files. (3) Test ids and the E0200 to E0500 catalogue entries need adding to spec/errors.json as the RFC says.
- tester: approve. The one decision is to remove the fail mark (RFC 0104 G10, rule F7, E0403). Against the purpose clause it saves tokens: tools.check.tokens gives 36 for the sample without marks and 38 with them, and 3 vs 2 for the io probes, which matches the RFC's Cost table. Principle 3 names fail as a signature effect, but exempts marks that nearly every function would carry. Trapping is that case: every + - * can trap and wh traps on the step budget. TR1 keeps a trap defined (principle 7). I checked the test expectations I could derive by hand (arithmetic, MIN handling, digit-sum, short-circuit, shadowing, and the E0201, E0202, E0300, E0303 and E0400 spans) and found none wrong. Notes for revision, none blocking: (1) L2 lists the statement starters as let, var, put, if, wh or a name, but leaves out ret, which F4, C2 and the card all use as a statement. (2) Open question 1 says the lexical owner may drop fail as a keyword and that the RFC works either way. It does not: B5 and test kc-reserved-fail (E0003 for let fail=1) depend on fail being a keyword. (3) Accepted RFC 0093 lists put and fail among the E0300 reserved names. This RFC gives them E0003 per RFC 0104 G3 and does not say it amends 0093 for that case. I did not run the compiler; the RFC's tests are not in the tree yet.
